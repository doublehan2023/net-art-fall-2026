About the Exhibition

The Art of Us

This exhibition considers how networked spaces turn viewers into participants. The World's First Collaborative Sentence, The Sheep Market, and Ten Thousand Cents each gather many small online actions into an artwork that no single contributor could make alone.

Douglas Davis's open-ended sentence treats the web as a shared writing surface: visitors voluntarily add language, changing the work as they encounter it. Aaron Koblin's projects make a more uneasy kind of collective image. The Sheep Market assembles 10,000 sheep drawn through Amazon Mechanical Turk, while Ten Thousand Cents asks 10,000 anonymous workers to redraw fragments of a $100 bill.

Together, these works celebrate the surprising texture of distributed creativity while asking who benefits from participation. They distinguish voluntary contribution from paid digital labor, where a vast image can be built from work valued at only a few cents. The Art of Us invites viewers to see online collaboration as both a generous social form and a system shaped by labor, value, and visibility.
